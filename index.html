VANTAGE

Hackathon, Sep 2026

A customer asks for a part in their own words. The supplier gets the right product back, or the one question it needs to ask.

Why it matters

Today a salesperson searches a huge catalogue by hand for every request. One wrong detail means the wrong part gets shipped, so a fast answer only helps if it is right.

What it does

It reads the request, looks for matching products and checks every detail it can. Then it suggests the product, asks one question if something is missing, or stops if the details contradict each other.

My part

I built the matching logic in Python and tested how often it is right. Jeet Upadhyay built the screens. The challenge came from Boie GmbH, an industrial parts supplier.

Why the AI does not decide

A guess is worse than no answer when a wrong part costs money. I tested two AI models for reading the requests. The smaller one made up about 4 in 10 details, like a size or a part number. The larger one made up about 1 in 100, and simple rules still found more right parts. So simple rules make the final choice, and the same request always gets the same answer. That is what lets a sales team trust it.

Next

Boie invited us to their site in October to talk about a version for their own systems.

Storymap Console

Self initiated, Aug 2026

Editors see the real map while they write, and mistakes are caught before a story goes live. Built to apply to die wegmeister, a company that makes interactive map stories.

Why it matters

The company wants one map story to take 30 minutes instead of 5 hours. I guessed that most of that time goes into moving the map, checking it and fixing it again, so that is the part I made faster.

What I built

One screen with the text on the left and the live map on the right. One click copies the current map view into the story, and checks warn about wrong locations, missing image descriptions and empty translations before saving.

Result

81 edits in a row without reloading the page. die wegmeister liked it, and it is now a reference in the student challenge they run with TUM.

Preflight

Self initiated, Sep 2026

Before a company pays for an analysis, it finds out whether its data can answer the question at all.

Why it matters

Presage, a CRM startup, checks whether a customer has enough data before working with them, but not whether that data can show cause and effect. Finding that out late wastes the customer's time and trust.

What I built

Seven simple checks in Python. Each one says green, amber or red, explains why, and says what to fix. A person can check every result by hand.

EV spare parts forecasting

University, Jul 2026

Why the most accurate forecast cost about 8 times more.

We forecast demand for 24 electric vehicle spare parts over 78 weeks, with five methods. The most accurate one cost about 8 times more than a less accurate forecast with a small safety stock, because running out of a part costs far more than keeping one extra.

University group project of five (Programming for Management Studies, TUM). I led the forecasting.
